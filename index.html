How it works

BiggerBrother uses computer vision to analyse your training videos frame by frame. Upload a clip, and our pose estimation model tracks 33 body landmarks across every frame.

From those landmarks we calculate rep count, concentric and eccentric tempo, bar velocity, range of motion, and movement symmetry — all without any wearable sensors.

Step 1: Upload a video

Select a video file (MP4 or MOV, up to 500 MB) of your set. Choose between a normal upload for standard analysis or a calibration upload if you want absolute measurements and RIR estimation. No account or sign-up is required.

Step 2: Pose tracking

MediaPipe Pose detects 33 landmarks per frame — shoulders, hips, knees, ankles, wrists, and more. The model runs at 30+ fps, giving us a detailed movement timeline for your entire set.

Step 3: Rep detection & metrics

Peak-and-trough analysis on the primary joint angle identifies each rep. For each rep we calculate concentric/eccentric tempo, peak velocity, range of motion, and bar path deviation. A composite form score aggregates symmetry, depth, and bar control.

Step 4: RIR estimation

If you've uploaded a calibration set (taken close to failure), BiggerBrother fits a velocity-load curve to your data. Future sets are compared against this curve to estimate how many reps you had left in the tank.

Camera setup

For best results, follow these guidelines when recording:

  • Static tripod. Keep the camera stationary — do not use a moving or handheld shot.
  • Side-on angle. Position the camera perpendicular to the lifter’s plane of motion. A 45-degree front-side angle works for some exercises, but pure lateral is preferred.
  • Full body in frame. Ensure the entire body is visible from head to toe throughout the entire set. Cropped frames reduce landmark accuracy.
  • Good lighting. Well-lit scenes produce significantly better landmark detection. Avoid strong backlight, shadows across the body, or dark clothing that blends into the background.
  • No obstructions. Keep the lifter’s body and the barbell/dumbbell fully visible. Do not let spotters, racks, or other equipment block the view.
  • Frame rate. 30 fps or higher is recommended for accurate velocity and tempo calculations.

Calibration (for absolute measurements)

A calibration video establishes a real-world reference scale so BiggerBrother can report absolute velocity (m/s) and absolute range of motion (m). Without calibration, metrics are reported in relative/arbitrary units.

How to record a calibration clip

  1. Place a known reference length (e.g., a 45 cm foam roller or a 20 kg barbell that is exactly 1.2 m between collars) in the same plane as the lifter.
  2. Record a 5-second static clip with the reference object visible alongside the lifter’s full body.
  3. Upload this clip using the Upload Calibration Set option in the app.

The calibration also creates a personalised velocity-load curve for RIR estimation. The curve fits a 2nd-degree polynomial to the velocity and rep-number data from your calibration set.

Metrics glossary

Every metric on the BiggerBrother dashboard is defined below. Values shown are per-set averages unless stated otherwise.

Total reps
The total number of repetitions detected from the joint-angle time series. Peaks and troughs in the primary movement angle (e.g., knee angle for squats, elbow angle for curls) define rep boundaries.
Avg tempo
Average duration of a single rep, in seconds. Broken into concentric (lifting) and eccentric (lowering) phases. Example: “3.1s — 0.2s eccentric” means the lowering phase is 0.2 seconds slower than average, indicating controlled descent.
Peak velocity
The maximum concentric bar velocity observed across all reps, in m/s. Higher peak velocity at a given weight typically indicates lower fatigue. Typical ranges: squat 0.4–0.8 m/s at 70% 1RM, bench press 0.3–0.7 m/s.
Avg depth / range of motion
Measured in degrees for joint angles (e.g., knee angle at the bottom of a squat) or metres for absolute bar displacement. “Below parallel” means the hip crease dropped below the top of the knee.
Bar path deviation
Lateral displacement of the barbell from a straight vertical line, in cm. A deviation below 5 cm is considered good for most compound lifts. Higher deviation indicates bar wobble, often caused by muscle imbalance or fatigue.
Concentric power
Estimated power output during the concentric phase, in watts. Derived from weight × concentric velocity. Useful for tracking explosive strength development.
Tempo consistency
Expressed as a percentage. 100% means every rep was exactly the same duration. Above 90% is excellent; below 70% suggests pacing problems or form breakdown.
Left/right balance
Ratio of movement contribution between left and right sides, expressed as a percentage split. 50/50 is perfectly symmetrical. A split of 60/40 or worse may indicate an underlying muscle imbalance.
Form score
A composite score (0–100) that aggregates depth consistency, bar path deviation, and movement symmetry. The score is calculated as the weighted sum of these three sub-scores. A score above 80 is considered good form; above 90 is excellent.
Reps in reserve (RIR)
An estimate of how many additional reps the lifter could have completed before reaching failure. Only available after a calibration set has been uploaded. Uses the fitted velocity-load curve to compare current rep velocities against the failure profile.
Estimated 1RM
Estimated one-rep maximum based on the relationship between rep velocity and load. More accurate when multiple loads and rep speeds are available from previous sessions.
Mean concentric speed
Average bar velocity during the concentric phase only (not averaged across the entire rep). Measured in m/s. This is the primary input to the RIR estimation model.

Frequently asked questions

How is my video data handled?

Videos are uploaded to the server, processed in memory, and deleted immediately after analysis. No video data is stored, cached, or logged. Pose landmark coordinates are ephemeral and not persisted beyond the HTTP request lifecycle.

What file formats and sizes are supported?

MP4 and MOV files up to 500 MB. For longer sets, lower the resolution or trim the clip before uploading. A typical 30-second 1080p clip is well under the limit.

Can I use BiggerBrother on my phone?

Yes. The web app is fully responsive and works in mobile browsers. Record with your phone’s camera, then upload directly from your camera roll. Ensure the phone is on a tripod or propped against a stable surface.

Why is my form score lower than expected?

The form score penalises bar path wobble, asymmetrical movement, and inconsistent depth. Common reasons: uneven foot placement, fatigue-induced drift in later reps, or an off-centre camera angle that makes the bar appear to deviate. Check your camera alignment before re-recording.

How accurate is the RIR estimate?

RIR accuracy depends on calibration quality. A calibration set taken close to actual failure produces a more reliable velocity-load curve. RIR estimates are most accurate within 0–3 reps of failure. The model is a 2nd-degree polynomial fit and may underperform on exercises with high technical complexity (e.g., deadlifts).

What is considered a “good” peak velocity for my weight?

As a rough guide: at 70% of your 1RM, expect squat concentric peak velocity between 0.50–0.80 m/s, bench press between 0.35–0.65 m/s, and overhead press between 0.30–0.55 m/s. Values vary significantly by individual technique and training status.

Can I use it for dumbbell exercises?

Currently the tool is optimised for barbell exercises. Dumbbell tracking is on the roadmap but not yet supported. Bicep curls with dumbbells partially work since the tracking is joint-angle based, but absolute bar path metrics are not meaningful without a single rigid bar.

Troubleshooting

“No landmarks detected” error
The pose model could not find a person in the video. Ensure the full body is visible, lighting is adequate, and the video is at least 480p resolution.
Rep count seems incorrect
Rep detection uses prominence-based peak finding. Very slow, partial, or pause-rep squats may not register clean peaks. Ensure each rep includes a clear descent and ascent without prolonged pauses at the bottom.
Velocity values look wrong
Without calibration, velocity is reported in arbitrary units. Upload a calibration clip with a known reference length for absolute m/s values. Also verify you selected the correct exercise — velocity heuristics are exercise-specific.
Upload fails or times out
File size limit is 500 MB. Check your network speed. If the issue persists, reduce video resolution or trim the clip to under 60 seconds.
